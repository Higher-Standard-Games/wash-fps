# Campaign

The campaign is a story of eight operations played against bots. Open it with
**Campaign** in the lobby's menu, under **Quick play**. The key's hint shows how
many operations you have cleared.

## The operations page

The page opens on the story so far: Neo-Kyoto, and you, an unranked runner
(one line of it on a short screen). The operations run down the page in
order, each with its comic card and, beside it, its part of the story, its
mode and the way in. A track down the left joins them: cyan through the
operations you have cleared, gold at the one to play next, dashed beyond. The
page opens at your next operation; scroll for the rest. The count at the top
shows how many you have cleared, with a segment for each operation: cyan once
cleared, gold for the next.

Under the last operation the track runs on to the finale. It stays
classified until every operation is cleared; then it shows the closing comic
under a **Campaign complete!** plate, larger than the other cards and turning
slowly. Click it to read it.

Click an operation's comic card to read it one panel at a time, as before a
mission, without starting it; click or press a key to move on, **Esc** to
close it.

| On the track | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Cyan ✓ | Cleared. Shows its game mode and the most frags you scored in a win. **Replay** plays it again. |
| Gold number | The one to play next, with its game mode and **Play**. |
| Grey | Classified: clear the operation before it to unlock it. |

**Survival** (Bot Trap): you start with the blade and a machine gun, and arm
yourself from the map, and you heal a point a second up to 100. Last the five
minutes to clear it. You get one respawn: your second death ends the match. The robots come armed in turn: one with a machine gun
it can aim, the next with a machine gun it can only fire from the hip, the next
with a grenade launcher, and round again; they pick nothing up. Every robot you kill brings the next, and they get more and
tougher each minute:

| Minute | Robots at once | Health of a robot spawning then |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 4 | 30 |
| 2 | 5 | 40 |
| 3 | 6 | 50 |
| 4-5 | 7 | 50 |

**Road's End** is a team deathmatch, one against two, with no frag limit: it
runs the full ten minutes, and the side ahead at the end wins. A tie plays
on in one-minute overtimes until a side leads.

## Music

The operations page has its own song, the campaign opener, looping. Opening
the page fades the lobby's music out and the opener in; leaving it, the
lobby's song comes back from where it paused, and the opener carries on from
where it paused next time. Four operations play a song of their own in place
of the match music: Cartel Contract plays *Cartel Dreams*, Bot Trap the
campaign opener, and Siege Breaker and Road's End *Boss Fight*, each looping. The others, and every match
outside the campaign, play the usual match music. The lobby's speaker key
silences the operations page's music too, but not Bot Trap's.

## Playing an operation

**Play** fades to black and plays the operation's comic card: one
panel at a time fills the screen while the rest of the card is blurred. A panel
moves on by itself after five seconds; click or press any key to move on
sooner, or **Esc** to skip the rest. Meanwhile the game sets up a private room
with the operation's map and bots, and the match loads as soon as the card is
done.

There is no warmup and no ready-up: as soon as you enter, a 10-second
countdown starts ("get ready", then three, two, one), with everyone held at
their spawn until it reaches zero. Over it, a yellow briefing in the upper left
says what the operation asks: its goal, a line of story, and the rules that
decide it.

In a campaign match you play the robot, and your opponents wear the operation's
model (the Cartel King is the cartel model, the riot cops are police), whatever
your **Player models** settings say. Your crew in Concrete Strike is a mix of
cartel and riotkitty. Teammates are always drawn green, tinted and outlined,
whatever your colour, tint and outline settings.

Concrete Strike is a sabotage run: your side attacks in every round, with no
swap at the half. The first side to win 10 rounds wins the match, by any
margin: 10-0 at the quickest, or 10-9. The match itself is an ordinary match of its
mode; when it ends, **Back to the lobby** brings you back to the operations page.

Win the match to clear the operation and unlock the next one. In free for all,
winning means finishing first, ahead of everyone; a tie for first is a draw. A
loss or a draw clears nothing: **Retry** on the end screen plays the operation
again at once, without its comic card. Like any private match against bots,
a campaign match counts toward your record and rating, except Bot Trap and
Road's End: Survival and team deathmatch have no rating of their own.

A campaign match is yours alone: nobody can join it midway or watch it.

## Campaign demos

Campaign matches are recorded like any other. Their demos are not in the shared
**Latest** list or the featured picks; you find them under **Your demos**, for
replays and highlight videos.

## Where progress is kept

Progress belongs to your account: the lobby keeps it, so it follows your
account key to any browser. The browser keeps a copy so the page shows your
progress at once.
